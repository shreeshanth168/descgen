10 Product Description Mistakes That Are Costing You Sales

DescGen AI Blog · Guides

Most underperforming product listings aren't failing because of the product — they're failing because of small, fixable writing habits that quietly discourage buyers. Here are the ten most common ones.

1. Listing Features Without Explaining Why They Matter

"304 stainless steel" means little to most shoppers on its own. "304 stainless steel — won't rust or hold odors, even after years of daily use" tells them why it's worth caring about.

2. Writing for Every Platform the Same Way

Copy tuned for Etsy's warm, story-driven tone will underperform if pasted directly into an Amazon listing, and vice versa. Buyer expectations differ by platform, and generic copy reads as generic everywhere.

3. Burying the Most Important Detail

Shoppers decide fast, often from the first sentence or first bullet. If your strongest selling point is in paragraph three, most buyers never see it.

4. Ignoring Mobile Formatting

Long unbroken paragraphs that look fine on desktop often become a dense, unreadable wall of text on a phone screen — where the majority of shopping now happens.

5. Overusing Vague Superlatives

Words like "amazing," "best-in-class," or "revolutionary" without any supporting detail tend to reduce credibility rather than build it. Specific claims outperform generic enthusiasm.

6. Leaving Common Questions Unanswered

Sizing, materials, care instructions, and what's included in the box are the questions that most often stop a purchase or trigger a pre-sale message. Answering them directly in the description reduces friction.

7. Inconsistency Between Description and Specifications

When the written description and the specifications table don't match, it damages trust and increases returns — buyers notice these mismatches more often than sellers expect.

8. Never Updating Old Listings

A listing written once and never revisited tends to fall behind as buyer expectations, keywords, and even the product itself evolve. Periodic small updates keep listings both accurate and more visible in search.

9. Skipping Keywords Buyers Actually Search

Writing only in brand language ("our signature blend") instead of the terms real buyers search for ("organic loose leaf green tea") means the listing may never surface for the searches that would find it.

10. Making Claims That Can't Be Verified

Overstated claims — about durability, health benefits, or performance — that don't hold up after purchase are one of the most common sources of returns and negative reviews. Accurate, honest copy converts better over time than copy that oversells.

Avoiding all ten of these manually, across every listing, is a lot to track.

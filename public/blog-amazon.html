How to Write Amazon Bullet Points and Titles That Convert

DescGen AI Blog · Marketplace Tips

Amazon shoppers make purchase decisions fast — often from the title and bullet points alone, without ever scrolling to the full description. Getting these two elements right matters more on Amazon than on almost any other marketplace.

1. Structure Your Title for Both Search and Skimming

A strong Amazon title typically follows a pattern: brand, product type, key differentiator, then size or quantity. Avoid front-loading with adjectives like "Best" or "Premium" — Amazon's algorithm and shoppers both respond better to concrete, specific information placed early in the title.

2. Lead Each Bullet With the Benefit

The most common mistake in Amazon bullets is starting with a feature instead of a benefit. "600 THREAD COUNT" tells a shopper a fact; "SLEEPS COOLER — 600 thread count for breathable, all-night comfort" tells them why it matters. Capitalizing the first few words of each bullet also improves scannability.

3. Cover Different Objections in Each Bullet

Rather than repeating similar points, use your five bullets strategically — one for quality/materials, one for size/fit, one for use cases, one for what's included, and one for a guarantee or unique selling point. This covers more of a shopper's decision-making surface area than five bullets saying similar things.

4. Don't Waste Backend Search Terms

Amazon gives you a hidden backend keyword field that doesn't appear on the listing itself. Use it for synonyms, alternate spellings, and related search terms you didn't fit naturally into your title or bullets — but avoid repeating words already in your title, since that space is limited and repetition wastes it.

5. Match Bullet Length to Mobile Screens

A large share of Amazon shoppers browse on mobile, where bullets wrap awkwardly if they run too long. Aiming for roughly 150-200 characters per bullet tends to display cleanly across both desktop and mobile.

6. Keep Claims Verifiable

Amazon has strict policies against unsubstantiated claims (like "clinically proven" without evidence). Stick to descriptions you can back up — this also happens to build more genuine trust with buyers reading closely.

Writing five distinct, benefit-led bullets for every SKU adds up fast.
